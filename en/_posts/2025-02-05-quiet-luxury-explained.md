## Quiet luxury explained: the definition, then why it came back

Quiet luxury has a one-line definition: understated design, high-quality materials and classic tailoring, with no visible logos or other open markers of wealth. It signals money through restraint instead of branding.

### Quiet luxury, stealth wealth, old money: three different things

The terms get swapped around, but they point at different motives. Stealth wealth means hiding money. Quiet luxury means signalling it discreetly. Old money means wealth held for at least two generations. One doesn't want to be seen; one wants to be seen by the right people.

### The history is older than the hashtag

In the late 18th and early 19th centuries the new capitalist class in Europe and America dressed plainly to set itself apart from courtly excess. *The Great Gatsby* (1925) stages the same contrast: the Buchanans' restraint against Gatsby's pink suit.

In the 1990s Donna Karan and Miuccia Prada carried the line forward with practical clothes for working women. Jil Sander and Giorgio Armani, in the 1980s and 90s, are usually named as the precursors.

### The 2020s revival: a TV series and a court case

Two things did it. HBO's *Succession* turned "Succession core" into a search term. Then in March 2023 Gwyneth Paltrow spent a week in a Utah courtroom over a ski collision, and every unbranded knit and coat she wore was analysed piece by piece. By July the New York Times' Guy Trebay had written the trend up.

The academic explanation came earlier. A 2015 study by Eckhardt and colleagues found that as "masstige" brands flood the street with logos, the affluent move toward subtler forms of distinction.

By January 2024 the Wall Street Journal was calling the term over. The term, not the clothes.

### The brands, and one detail about The Row

Loro Piana, Brunello Cucinelli, Hermès, Bottega Veneta and The Row come up most. They share a focus on material and construction, styles that ignore trends, almost no logos, and scarcity through price.

The Row was founded in 2006 by Mary-Kate and Ashley Olsen, starting from Ashley's search for a perfect T-shirt. The first collection had seven pieces; Barneys bought all of them. For three years the founders gave no interviews. The name comes from Savile Row.

Loro Piana started in Quarona, Italy, in 1924 and turned a hundred in 2024.
