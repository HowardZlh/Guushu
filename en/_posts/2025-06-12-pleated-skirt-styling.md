## How to style a pleated skirt: a summer piece since the 1919 Wimbledon final

Start with why the pleated skirt comes back every summer. At the 1919 Wimbledon women's final, Suzanne Lenglen walked out in a short-sleeved blouse and a calf-length pleated skirt, in a stadium full of ankle-length dresses and corsets. From 1920 Jean Patou designed her outfits, and the hem rose to the knee. Movement and room to move: the skirt's two selling points were first seen on a tennis court.

Still, it reads crisp. In motion, it flies. The pleats give the body space and the fold lines shape the silhouette.

### Office: fine pressed pleats, quiet top

A midi skirt in fine pressed pleats, a plain knit tank, loafers. Sharp folds below, calm above. Businesslike without stiffness.

### Weekend: accordion pleats and a white T-shirt

An accordion-pleated skirt, a white tee, flat shoes. Let the skirt do the moving; the simpler the top, the better. Vogue's March 2025 trend list called this category "buoyant cotton skirts."

### Evening: sheen and a thin strap

A pleated skirt in a fabric with some shine, a camisole, heeled sandals. Pleats under lights move by themselves and bring a bit of occasion with them.

![Pleated skirt summer outfit: accordion pleats with a white T-shirt](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1762342676026-09e25daaf607?w=800)

### Simple on top

The skirt already carries a lot of information, so keep the top plain: a fitted knit, a basic shirt, a solid tank. For depth, stay in one colour family and vary the shade. Tucking the top in lengthens the lower half.

### Three lengths

- **Knee.** The most flexible, office or occasion. Also Lenglen's length after 1920.
- **Midi.** The summer default.
- **Ankle.** The most movement; best for holidays and evenings.

### A detail borrowed from the kilt

A kilt uses knife or box pleats, and the maker pleats "to the stripe" or "to the sett" so the tartan runs continuously across the folds. Use that as a quality check on any checked pleated skirt: if the pattern lines up on the back of each pleat, someone took care.

### Care

Keep pressed pleats away from high heat and heavy pressure. Hang them or roll them loosely. Read the label; synthetics usually survive washing and keep their fold better than natural fibres.
