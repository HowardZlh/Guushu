## Fashion month explained: four cities, four weeks, one season of clothes

Fashion month is the stretch when the four big fashion weeks run back to back. From mid-February the women's fall/winter 2026 ready-to-wear collections show in New York, London, Milan, Paris, about a week each, and the industry calls the whole run fashion month. It happens twice a year: fall/winter in February and March, spring/summer in September and October.

So what you see in February is what reaches stores in the fall.

### The order: New York, London, Milan, Paris

Wikipedia's Milan Fashion Week entry puts it plainly: "The schedule begins with New York, followed by London, and then Milan, and ending with Paris." The four are known as the Big Four. New York was the first city to hold a fashion week, but the birth years don't match the running order.

| City | First edition | Organized by |
|---|---|---|
| New York | July 19, 1943, as "Press Week" | Eleanor Lambert, New York Dress Institute |
| Milan | June 11, 1958 | Camera Sindacale della Moda Italiana, forerunner of today's CNMI |
| Paris | October 1 to 9, 1973 | Fédération Française de la Couture |
| London | 1984 | British Fashion Council |

### 1943: why New York went first

In 1943, with the war on, Eleanor Lambert was press director of the New York Dress Institute, the American fashion industry's first promotional body. On July 19 she staged the first Press Week, and the stated aim was to pull attention away from French fashion.

It took until 1993 for the CFDA to gather New York's shows in one place. Bryant Park tents from 1994 to 2009, Lincoln Center from 2010 to 2015, then a scattering across the city, with Spring Studios as the main venue today.

### 1984: London opens in a car park

London Fashion Week's first edition was in 1984, in the car park of the Commonwealth Institute in Kensington: 15 shows, with Vivienne Westwood, Betty Jackson and Ghost on the list. In spring 2010 it became the first of the Big Four to stream shows live online. In September 2018 it became the first major fashion week to go fur-free.

### Paris has rules a century older than its fashion week

Paris Fashion Week dates from 1973, but the couture rulebook goes back to 1868 and the Chambre Syndicale de la Haute Couture. Under the 1945 rules a couture house had to present at least 35 looks a season, covering both day and evening. Couture always shows before ready-to-wear.

### Why show six months early

After a show, buyers order, factories produce, goods ship. Each step takes months, and showing a season ahead leaves room for all of it. A runway show is a preview for the trade more than a shop window for the public.

See-now-buy-now tried to break that rhythm. In 2016 Tom Ford, Tommy Hilfiger and Moschino among others moved to in-season shows, and Burberry announced its switch that February. By 2017 the French federation had declined to adopt it. The advance calendar is still the norm.

### Two signals before fall/winter 2026 opened

On January 29, 2026, ahead of the Big Four, Toteme showed fall 2026 to about 50 guests in its Paris showroom. Vogue's Tiziana Cardini wrote of "no theatrics, just clothes presented with confident restraint" and all-white looks "that recalled the hush of a Swedish winter."

The other came from New York. On December 3, 2025, the CFDA announced that official fashion week events would be fur-free from September 2026.

### How to watch it if you're not a buyer

- **Silhouette over single pieces.** A season's signal is in the shoulder line, the waist, the length, not in one hard-to-wear look.
- **Repetition.** When one color, fabric or shape turns up at several houses, it usually filters down to the next season's shelves.
- **The diluted version.** The runway is the loud edit; what reaches your closet is quieter.
